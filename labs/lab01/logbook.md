# Lab 1: experiment logbook

One row per run that trains a model; preprocessing and data-analysis decisions are in the notebook and in the report. Budget: 24 runs out of the ~25 allowed (Task 5: 10, Task 6: 6). Validation is the split used to choose every model; test is only used in 5D and in the final Task 6 verdict.

| Run | Task | Change (one thing) | Hypothesis before running | Key result (validation) | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | 2A | First baseline: 1 hidden layer of 64 units, ReLU, AdamW 5e-4, batch 64, <=100 epochs, early stopping. | PortScan is the easiest class (its flows share a port pattern); Brute Force is the hardest because it is the rarest (4.9%) and its flows are short. | macro-F1 0.983, acc 0.992, best epoch 96/100 | Confirmed only in part: Brute Force has the lowest F1 (0.953) but a recall of 0.995, so rarity does not cost recalls; the low F1 comes from precision. The model can exploit port 80. |
| R2 (<-R1) | 2B | S1: train on raw, unscaled features; everything else as 2A. | Features span 1e0 to 1e9, so the gradient is badly conditioned: slower, noisier descent and a worse plateau. | macro-F1 0.921, acc 0.953, best epoch 46/56, BF-F1 0.846 | Confirmed: validation macro-F1 falls from 0.983 to 0.921 (-0.062). It is the least harmful of the three failures, because AdamW adapts the step per parameter, yet the loss stays in the thousands and the F1 swings between epochs: scaling is still the first fundamental to fix. |
| R3 (<-R1) | 2B | S2: learning rate 5e-4 -> 0.5 (1000x). | The steps will overshoot the minimum: the loss oscillates, never settles and early stopping fires after a few epochs. | macro-F1 0.910, acc 0.953, best epoch 5/15, BF-F1 0.770 | Confirmed: the best epoch is 5 and the run stops after ~15 epochs. Macro-F1 0.910 (-0.073) is nominally above S3's, but the gaps among S1, S2 and S3 are below the noise threshold: their ranking is not established. BF recall 0.836 as in R2, R4 and R6 (the same 35 flows are missed). |
| R4 (<-R1) | 2B | S3: learning rate 5e-4 -> 5e-6 (100x smaller). | The loss will fall almost linearly and still be falling when the 100-epoch budget ends. | macro-F1 0.893, acc 0.933, best epoch 100/100, BF-F1 0.814 | Confirmed: the best epoch is the last one (100) and the curve is still decreasing. Macro-F1 0.893 (-0.090) is nominally the worst of the three, but not distinguishable from S1 and S2. |
| R5 (<-R1) | 2C | 300 epochs instead of 100, everything else as 2A. | The 2A run was still improving at epoch 96 of 100, so a longer budget should give a small gain. | macro-F1 0.983, acc 0.992, best epoch 156/166 | No measurable gain: the best value is the same (0.983 macro-F1). The model had already converged; the extra epochs only make the baseline more stable. Kept as the baseline to beat. |
| R6 (<-R5) | 2C | Activation ReLU -> linear, on the 300-epoch baseline. | The port-80 attack classes are separated by a threshold; a linear model should lose more on them than on the other classes. | macro-F1 0.922, acc 0.962, best epoch 93/103 | Confirmed: macro-F1 falls to 0.922 and Brute Force F1 to 0.797. The non-linearity pays exactly on the classes that a single linear cut cannot separate. |
| R7 (<-R5) | 4 | Retrain the baseline on the port-free, de-duplicated data (D2), same hyper-parameters. | PortScan will suffer most: after removing the port and the duplicates only 285 distinct flows are left (1.3% of D2). | macro-F1 0.959, acc 0.982, best epoch 167/177, PortScan recall 0.907 | Partly confirmed: macro-F1 drops from 0.983 to 0.959 and PortScan recall to 0.907, but the class is still detected. The loss of the port costs more than the loss of the duplicates. |
| R8 (<-R7) | 4 | Class-weighted cross-entropy (weights from the training split), same model. | Accuracy will fall, macro-F1 will rise; rare-class recall up, rare-class precision down. | macro-F1 0.884, acc 0.960, best epoch 81/91, PortScan recall 0.977 | Only the second half held: accuracy 0.982 -> 0.960 and macro-F1 0.959 -> 0.884 both fall, while PortScan recall rises to 0.977 and its precision drops to 0.553. Reweighting buys recall, not macro-F1 on this data. |
| R9 (<-R8) | 5A | Two hidden layers (64, 32), weighted loss, 300 epochs. | One more layer can model interactions between rates and flag counts, so a small gain over the single layer. | macro-F1 0.901, acc 0.963, best epoch 32/42 | Not distinguishable from noise: macro-F1 0.884 -> 0.901 (+0.017), below 0.05. The sign matches the hypothesis, the size does not allow a conclusion. |
| R10 (<-R9) | 5A | Three hidden layers (64, 32, 16). | A third, narrower layer adds capacity where the data is low-dimensional; expect no gain and a risk of underfitting. | macro-F1 0.897, acc 0.962, best epoch 33/43 | Within noise: 0.897, -0.004 against the two-layer net. Depth alone does not help, and does not hurt measurably. |
| R11 (<-R10) | 5A | Three wider layers (128, 64, 32). | Width, not depth, is what pays: the wider first layer keeps more of the 15 features and should beat (64, 32, 16). | macro-F1 0.925, acc 0.969, best epoch 22/32 | Not distinguishable from noise: 0.925, the highest of the weighted runs of Task 5, but only +0.028 over R10 and +0.041 over R8. Width is not shown to matter; all Task 5 runs from R8 stay below R7. |
| R12 (<-R8) | 5B | Batch size 16. | Many noisy updates per epoch: better generalization, much slower wall-clock time per epoch. | macro-F1 0.899, acc 0.965, best epoch 50/60 | Time confirmed (40.7 s, the slowest). Generalization not distinguishable from noise: 0.899 is +0.031 over R14 and +0.033 over R13, below 0.05. |
| R13 (<-R8) | 5B | Batch size 1024. | Few updates per epoch: fastest epochs, but many more epochs needed and slightly worse generalization. | macro-F1 0.866, acc 0.954, best epoch 293/300 | Time and epochs confirmed: 300 epochs to reach 0.866. The score is the lowest of the three but within 0.002 of R14, so noise. Part of any gap is the much smaller number of weight updates, not the batch size alone. |
| R14 (<-R8) | 5B | Batch size 128. | Middle ground: should land between 16 and 1024, close to the batch-64 baseline. | macro-F1 0.868, acc 0.957, best epoch 99/109 | Not confirmed: 0.868, between batch 16 and batch 1024, only 0.002 above the largest batch. All three batch sizes are within noise of one another; only the times differ. |
| R15 (<-R8) | 5C | SGD, lr 0.01, no momentum; weight decay 0.01 (the same as every other run). | Plain SGD will need many more epochs than AdamW and will plateau lower. | macro-F1 0.787, acc 0.906, best epoch 120/130 | Confirmed: 0.787 after 130 epochs, the lowest score of Task 5. The L2 penalty may be part of the cost, but this is untested: no SGD run has weight decay 0. |
| R16 (<-R15) | 5C | SGD, lr 0.1 (10x), everything else as R15. | Ten times the learning rate should close most of the gap and converge in far fewer epochs. | macro-F1 0.817, acc 0.920, best epoch 17/27 | 0.817, +0.030 over R15: below the noise threshold, so only the number of epochs (27 instead of 130) is a solid effect of the 10x learning rate. |
| R17 (<-R15) | 5C | SGD + momentum 0.9, lr 0.01. | Momentum should help at the badly chosen learning rate, but less than fixing the learning rate itself. | macro-F1 0.781, acc 0.905, best epoch 60/70 | Not confirmed: 0.781, -0.006 versus R15 (noise). Momentum at this learning rate buys nothing measurable. |
| R18 (<-R8) | 5C | AdamW learning rate 5e-4 -> 5e-3 (R8 is already AdamW; the comparison with the SGD runs changes optimizer and lr together). | AdamW with a 10x larger lr should match or beat tuned SGD. | macro-F1 0.897, acc 0.964, best epoch 21/31 | 0.897: +0.013 over R8 (noise), +0.080 over the best SGD run (above 0.05). The AdamW learning rate does not matter here; the optimizer family does, but AdamW against SGD cannot separate optimizer from learning rate. |
| R19 (<-R8) | 6A | 5 hidden layers (256, 128, 64, 32, 16), weighted loss as R8, weight decay 0, batch 128, 200 epochs, no regularization, no early stopping (weights of epoch 200 evaluated). | The capacity is far larger than the task: the training loss will keep falling while the validation loss flattens after a few dozen epochs. | macro-F1 0.951, acc 0.979, best epoch 44/200 | Partly confirmed: the training loss falls to 0.030, but the weighted validation loss does not flatten: minimum 0.077 at epoch 44, then it rises to 0.177. Unweighted final losses 0.058 (train) and 0.072 (validation); PortScan recall 1.000 on train vs 0.953 on validation. |
| R20 (<-R19) | 6B | Early stopping (patience 10, best weights restored) on the 6A setup. | Stopping at the validation minimum removes the last ~180 epochs with no loss of accuracy. | macro-F1 0.919, acc 0.965, best epoch 15/25 | Confirmed in time: the best epoch is 15 and the run stops at 25 (5.3 s instead of 39.6 s). Macro-F1 0.919 is 0.032 below 6A, within noise. It is the cheapest change of Task 6. |
| R21 (<-R20) | 6C | Weight decay 0.01 on top of early stopping. | L2 shrinks the weights and should reduce the train/validation gap slightly. | macro-F1 0.921, acc 0.967, best epoch 15/25 | 0.921, +0.002 over early stopping alone: no real effect at this scale. |
| R22 (<-R21) | 6D | Dropout 0.24 on top of weight decay. | Dropout should help more than weight decay on a small tabular set. | macro-F1 0.923, acc 0.969, best epoch 42/52 | 0.923, the best validation score of Task 6's regularized variants (+0.004 over 6B). Small, and within the noise of 43 PortScan flows. |
| R23 (<-R22) | 6E | Batch normalization added to the dropout configuration. | Batch normalization stabilizes training but should not add accuracy on this small network. | macro-F1 0.907, acc 0.964, best epoch 32/42 | Confirmed: 0.907, the lowest of the regularized variants. | R24 (<-R19) | 6F | Single 64-unit hidden layer, no regularization, 200 epochs (only the capacity changes). | The task is low-dimensional: a much smaller network should match 6A on validation. | macro-F1 0.923, acc 0.970, best epoch 117/200 | Capacity does not separate the runs: 0.923 against 0.951 for R19 (-0.028, noise).
