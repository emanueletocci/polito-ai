# Lab 1 - experiment logbook

One row per run that trains a model; preprocessing and data-analysis decisions are in the notebook and in the report. Budget: 24 runs out of the ~25 allowed (Task 5: 10, Task 6: 6). Validation is the split used to choose every model; test is only used in 5D and in the final Task 6 verdict.

| Run | Task | What I changed (one thing) | Hypothesis before running | Key result (validation) | What I concluded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | 2A | First baseline: 1 hidden layer of 64 units, ReLU, AdamW 5e-4, batch 64, <=100 epochs, early stopping. | PortScan is the easiest class (its flows share a port pattern); Brute Force is the hardest because it is the rarest (4.9%) and its flows are short. | macro-F1 0.983, acc 0.992, best epoch 96/100 | Confirmed only in part: Brute Force has the lowest F1 (0.953) but a recall of 0.995, so rarity does not cost recalls; the low F1 comes from precision. The model can exploit port 80. |
| R2 | 2B | S1: train on raw, unscaled features; everything else as 2A. | Features span 1e0 to 1e9, so the gradient is badly conditioned: slower, noisier descent and a worse plateau. | macro-F1 0.921, acc 0.953, best epoch 46/56, BF-F1 0.846 | Confirmed: validation macro-F1 falls from 0.983 to 0.921. Still far from catastrophic, because AdamW adapts the step per parameter; scaling remains the first fundamental to fix. |
| R3 | 2B | S2: learning rate 5e-4 -> 0.5 (1000x). | The steps will overshoot the minimum: the loss oscillates, never settles and early stopping fires after a few epochs. | macro-F1 0.910, acc 0.953, best epoch 5/15, BF-F1 0.770 | Confirmed: the best epoch is 5 and the run stops after ~15 epochs, but the final macro-F1 (0.910) is higher than S3's. A too-large lr is destructive, yet AdamW absorbs it better than expected. |
| R4 | 2B | S3: learning rate 5e-4 -> 5e-6 (100x smaller). | The loss will fall almost linearly and still be falling when the 100-epoch budget ends. | macro-F1 0.893, acc 0.933, best epoch 100/100, BF-F1 0.814 | Confirmed: the best epoch is the last one (100) and the curve is still decreasing. This is the worst of the three failures (macro-F1 0.893) because the model is far from converged. |
| R5 | 2C | 300 epochs instead of 100, everything else as 2A. | The 2A run was still improving at epoch 96 of 100, so a longer budget should give a small gain. | macro-F1 0.983, acc 0.992, best epoch 156/166 | No measurable gain: the best value is the same (0.983 macro-F1). The model had already converged; the extra epochs only make the baseline more stable. Kept as the baseline to beat. |
| R6 | 2C | Activation ReLU -> linear, on the 300-epoch baseline. | The port-80 attack classes are separated by a threshold; a linear model should lose more on them than on the other classes. | macro-F1 0.922, acc 0.962, best epoch 93/103 | Confirmed: macro-F1 falls to 0.922 and Brute Force F1 to 0.797. The non-linearity pays exactly on the classes that a single linear cut cannot separate. |
| R7 | 4 | Retrain the baseline on the port-free, de-duplicated data (D2), same hyper-parameters. | PortScan will suffer most: after removing the port and the duplicates only 285 distinct flows are left (1.3% of D2). | macro-F1 0.959, acc 0.982, best epoch 167/177, PortScan recall 0.907 | Partly confirmed: macro-F1 drops from 0.983 to 0.959 and PortScan recall to 0.907, but the class is still detected. The loss of the port costs more than the loss of the duplicates. |
| R8 | 4 | Class-weighted cross-entropy (weights from the training split), same model. | Accuracy will fall, macro-F1 will rise; rare-class recall up, rare-class precision down. | macro-F1 0.884, acc 0.960, best epoch 81/91, PortScan recall 0.977 | Only the second half held: accuracy 0.982 -> 0.960 and macro-F1 0.959 -> 0.884 both fall, while PortScan recall rises to 0.977 and its precision drops to 0.553. Reweighting buys recall, not macro-F1 on this data. |
| R9 | 5A | Two hidden layers (64, 32), weighted loss, 300 epochs. | One more layer can model interactions between rates and flag counts, so a small gain over the single layer. | macro-F1 0.901, acc 0.963, best epoch 32/42 | Confirmed: macro-F1 0.884 -> 0.901 (+0.017). Small but positive. |
| R10 | 5A | Three hidden layers (64, 32, 16). | A third, narrower layer adds capacity where the data is low-dimensional; expect no gain and a risk of underfitting. | macro-F1 0.897, acc 0.962, best epoch 33/43 | Confirmed: 0.897, slightly below the two-layer net. Depth alone does not help. |
| R11 | 5A | Three wider layers (128, 64, 32). | Width, not depth, is what pays: the wider first layer keeps more of the 15 features and should beat (64, 32, 16). | macro-F1 0.925, acc 0.969, best epoch 22/32 | Confirmed: 0.925, the best validation score of Task 5. Adding the third, narrower layer gives -0.004; widening the three layers gives +0.028. |
| R12 | 5B | Batch size 16. | Many noisy updates per epoch: better generalization, much slower wall-clock time per epoch. | macro-F1 0.899, acc 0.965, best epoch 50/60 | Confirmed on generalization (0.899 with the earliest best epoch); the run is also the slowest per epoch. |
| R13 | 5B | Batch size 1024. | Few updates per epoch: fastest epochs, but many more epochs needed and slightly worse generalization. | macro-F1 0.866, acc 0.954, best epoch 293/300 | Confirmed: 300 epochs to reach 0.866, the lowest of the three. Part of the gap is the much smaller number of weight updates, not the batch size alone. |
| R14 | 5B | Batch size 128. | Middle ground: should land between 16 and 1024, close to the batch-64 baseline. | macro-F1 0.868, acc 0.957, best epoch 99/109 | Not confirmed: 0.868, i.e. between batch 16 and batch 1024, only 0.002 above the largest batch. With 43 PortScan flows in validation the three scores are within noise. |
| R15 | 5C | SGD, lr 0.01, no momentum; weight decay 0.01 (the same as every other run). | Plain SGD will need many more epochs than AdamW and will plateau lower. | macro-F1 0.787, acc 0.906, best epoch 120/130 | Confirmed: 0.787 after 130 epochs, the lowest score of Task 5. Part of the cost is the L2 penalty: with this lr and only 130 epochs the model underfits. |
| R16 | 5C | SGD, lr 0.1 (10x), everything else as R15. | Ten times the learning rate should close most of the gap and converge in far fewer epochs. | macro-F1 0.817, acc 0.920, best epoch 17/27 | Confirmed: 0.817, +0.030 over R15, in 27 epochs instead of 130. Ten times the learning rate is the main lever inside SGD. |
| R17 | 5C | SGD + momentum 0.9, lr 0.01. | Momentum should help at the badly chosen learning rate, but less than fixing the learning rate itself. | macro-F1 0.781, acc 0.905, best epoch 60/70 | Not confirmed: 0.781, i.e. -0.006 versus R15. Momentum at this learning rate buys nothing; it cannot compensate for the small step. |
| R18 | 5C | AdamW, lr 5e-3 (changes both the optimizer and the lr). | AdamW with a 10x larger lr should match or beat tuned SGD. | macro-F1 0.897, acc 0.964, best epoch 21/31 | 0.897: +0.080 over the best SGD at the same weight decay. The run changes optimizer and learning rate together, so it cannot separate the two. |
| R19 | 6A | 5 hidden layers (256, 128, 64, 32, 16), batch 128, 200 epochs, no regularization, no early stopping. | The capacity is far larger than the task: the training loss will keep falling while the validation loss flattens after a few dozen epochs. | macro-F1 0.951, acc 0.979, best epoch 44/200 | Confirmed: the training loss falls to 0.058 and the validation loss flattens near 0.072, a gap of 0.014, with PortScan recall 1.000 on train vs 0.954 on validation. |
| R20 | 6B | Early stopping (patience 10, best weights restored) on the 6A setup. | Stopping at the validation minimum removes the last ~180 epochs with no loss of accuracy. | macro-F1 0.919, acc 0.965, best epoch 15/25 | Confirmed: the best epoch is 15 and the run stops at 25, with validation macro-F1 0.919 (0.032 below 6A). It is the cheapest change of Task 6. |
| R21 | 6C | Weight decay 0.01 on top of early stopping. | L2 shrinks the weights and should reduce the train/validation gap slightly. | macro-F1 0.921, acc 0.967, best epoch 15/25 | 0.921, +0.002 over early stopping alone: no real effect at this scale. |
| R22 | 6D | Dropout 0.24 on top of weight decay. | Dropout should help more than weight decay on a small tabular set. | macro-F1 0.923, acc 0.969, best epoch 42/52 | 0.923, the best validation score of Task 6's regularized variants (+0.004 over 6B). Small, and within the noise of 43 PortScan flows. |
| R23 | 6E | Batch normalization added to the dropout configuration. | Batch normalization stabilizes training but should not add accuracy on this small network. | macro-F1 0.907, acc 0.964, best epoch 32/42 | Confirmed: 0.907, the lowest of the regularized variants. It costs a run and helps nothing here. |
| R24 | 6F | Single 64-unit hidden layer, no regularization, 200 epochs (only the capacity changes). | The task is low-dimensional: a much smaller network should match 6A on validation. | macro-F1 0.923, acc 0.970, best epoch 117/200 | Confirmed on validation (0.923): capacity was never the bottleneck. On test it reaches 0.924 against 0.871 for the weighted baseline R8, but both stay below the unweighted R7 (0.957): the loss matters more than the architecture. |

Notes:
- Validation macro-F1 (4 classes) is the selection metric; accuracy is reported for completeness but is dominated by Benign (75%).
- Wall-clock times are printed by `run_experiment` and reported in the Task 5B answer; they are not part of the selection.
- Test is only touched in 5D and in the final Task 6 verdict, never to choose a model.
